How to read these results
  • Risk tiers come from keyword rules. A tier shows which topics a clause touches and how closely it is worth reading. It does not say whether a clause is unfair, unusual or enforceable, and nothing here is legal advice.
  • The rules can miss terms worded differently and can flag harmless mentions; "Why flagged" shows the exact phrases matched. "No flags" means no watched phrase was found, not that a clause is safe.
  • Offsets and the document text refer to the text extracted from the file after clean-up: page headers, footers and page numbers are removed and words hyphenated across lines are rejoined. It is not the PDF's page layout.
  • Section numbers can be missing when a PDF's font hides them. Such clauses show "§ —".
  • A paragraph whose last line reaches the page margin can merge with the next one. Clauses over {{ long_clause_chars }} characters are marked "long".
  • A heading applies to every clause after it until the next heading, so a document title or a "Last updated" line can appear as the heading of many clauses.
  • Numbered lists inside a clause, such as "1) … 2) …", may be split into separate clauses. Scanned PDFs have no text to extract (no OCR yet), and two-column layouts have not been tested.
